How it thinks

Junctions and roads

The city becomes points and links. Every road has a cost, and a jam makes that cost jump.

Traffic changes the best way

A jammed road costs several times more, so the cheapest trip can be a longer one.

Many stops, one loop

With several deliveries, the planner tries the possible orders and keeps the cheapest loop back to the depot.

A courier’s day

  1. 7:30Load the van. The depot’s list becomes the day’s stops.
  2. 8:15First loop planned. The planner picks the cheapest order.
  3. 9:40A jam appears. The road gets expensive and the route is planned again.
  4. 11:00A late order comes in. One more stop, one more recalculation.
  5. 13:30A road closes. The planner finds another way, or says the stop cannot be reached.
  6. 16:45Back at the depot. Compare the plan with what really happened.

With and without a planner

Without

  • Stops are driven in list order.
  • Drivers find jams by sitting in them.
  • A late order means guessing a new plan.
  • Nobody can say how much time was lost.

With Pathly

  • Stops are reordered into a short loop.
  • Jams raise a road’s cost before the van reaches it.
  • New orders and closures trigger a fresh plan.
  • Time saved is shown on every trip.

The method in plain words

  1. 1Draw the city as points (junctions) and links (roads).
  2. 2Give every road a cost. A clear block is cheap, a jammed block is not.
  3. 3Work out the cheapest way between every pair of stops.
  4. 4Try the possible stop orders and add up each loop.
  5. 5Keep the cheapest loop, drive it, and repeat when something changes.

Questions

Is the traffic real?

In the route planner, no: you draw the jams yourself on a made-up grid. The globe and the data board use real 2025 measurements from the TomTom Traffic Index.

Why does the route sometimes not change?

If a jam is not on the way, or going around costs more than sitting in it, the planner keeps the direct route.

What does “jams dodged” mean?

The number of jammed road blocks the traffic-blind route would drive through that the planned route avoids.

Why only four stops?

The demo checks every possible order. That is instant for four stops and grows very fast after that.

Does the Contact us form save my details?

No. It is a demo form, so nothing is sent or stored.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Tap intersections and roads on the map the same way you would click them.

About this project

This site is the working demo for a research project on intelligent logistics and route optimization. It shows how a computer can pick a delivery route and change it when conditions change.

The route planner’s city is a made-up grid, so its minutes and kilometers are simulated. The globe and the data board use real 2025 figures from the TomTom Traffic Index.
